Kimberly Maroon
A 20 Year Music Photography Archive

As a self taught photographer, live music was what captivated me in the beginning. The photographer I am today – chasing adrenaline pumping experiences, drawn to community and the power of personal stories – was shaped first by a decade following punk, rock, hardcore and metal artists. From house basements, dive bars and VFW halls to historic ballrooms and theatres, and a church basement, to amphitheaters, arenas, recording studios, and even a beloved 60s bowling alley — I was relentless for showing up, capturing the artists, the crowds, the crews, and the spaces that held us all. I saw bands explode, move on to form other acts, bands that toured through a narrow geographic radius, and ultimately without me knowing at the time — I became the designated record keeper.

The camera was the anchor in all the chaos, a way to contain all the energy, and a machine that reminded me that I was fearless. Loud Quiet Loud, a formula in music of a quiet verse and a loud chorus — The Pixies were known for it, then Nirvana — is reminiscent of how I felt photographing this music live. I disappeared behind the camera, and in an instant everything loud and turbulent became quiet and focused through the frame. Releasing this collection now as a memory palace, my hope is that it's loud again.
